# Ordinary RH through fourth-power samples

This is a working exposition, **not a completed standalone unconditional proof of RH**.

The main argument prints proofs of the prime-only analytic criterion and ending, the signed finite-group comparison, the conductor-one field and its degree, cyclotomic class-number oddness, strict descent at 3 and 7, integer-budget equivalence, finite-exception interval selection, and numerical absorption. The supporting arithmetic chapters print the characterwise minus descent, its cyclotomic derivative system and Frobenius selection.

This consolidation closes the odd prime-to-degree minus bound PB(E) by combining those internal proofs with the common Bernoulli integer. For conductor-one fields every odd prime is eligible. It also proves a uniform 24-generator bound for the unramified dyadic Galois module in the enlarged cyclotomic limit and its split quotient, with full bidual identifications giving the source's Tower.IsTower input, a full finite-twist cokernel identification and descent to the canonical CM field with restriction kernel length at most one, an exact centered prime-distribution identity, a spectral uniqueness lemma and the growth bound needed to apply it to zeta zeros. The dyadic argument bounds generators, not finite-twist orders.

The manuscript is still conditional on the uniform supply of signed geometric data. Anton's 552-line reduction assumes a uniform plus-height bound and a connected-family construction after Weber. His 630-line revision allows a weaker cubic-logarithmic height envelope. His subsequent two-character reduction isolates a simultaneous generic-prime sum and a pointwise bound on nontrivial characters at resonant or exceptional primes. This edition proves their full aggregation, trivial-character vanishing at 3 and 7 and at all resonant primes, and the cubic-logarithmic absorption. The two character estimates themselves remain assumptions. Its reported 3,857 imported project modules also contain substantial mathematical proofs; two explicit formal premises do not mean just two short prose lemmas remain.

The text still needs the uniform plus bound (or its weaker cubic-logarithmic version), uniform size bounds for the dyadic cokernels, the quantitative high-twist and exceptional-set estimates, and the required global theta, ramified matching, cusp/metric and integral-model constructions. The source review keeps these obligations explicit.
