\subsection{The actual kernel points and the adjoint-frame coefficient}
\label{subsec:faithful-geometric-kernel-count}

We retain the geometric hypotheses of
\texttt{ProperGroupGermFiniteSubscheme}.  Let \(E\) be a CM number
field, \(H\) its specified host, and \(O=\mathcal O_E\), with
\(\bar a=H.\mathrm{bar}(a)=a^*\).  Let \(k\) be algebraically closed
of characteristic the prime \(p\), and let \(t\) be the source's
polarized semiabelian datum \(\mathrm{SAB}(H,\operatorname{Spec}k)\).
Assume that its group \(G\) is proper and that its cubical line \(L\)
satisfies the source predicate \(\mathrm{KTriv}(G,L)\).
In particular \(G\) is an abelian variety.  Choose an actual
\(\mathrm{GermAt}(t,1,d)\), denoted \(g\).  Put
\[
 R_a=\operatorname{lin}_1(g.B.\mathrm{acta}(a)),\qquad
 C_a=\operatorname{bil}_{1,1}(g.B.\mathrm{ad}(a)).
\]
The doubled formal action is the actual action already fixed:
\[
 \rho_H(a)(u,f)=(R_a u,R_{\bar a}^{\,t}f-C_a^{\,t}u).
 \tag{KG1}\label{eq:faithful-kernel-doubled-action}
\]

Fix \(a\in O\).  Suppose a finite \(k\)-algebra \(A\), with a map
\(\kappa:\operatorname{Spec}A\to G\), represents the scheme-theoretic
kernel of \([a]\).  Thus \([a]\kappa=0\), and every point killed by
\([a]\) factors uniquely through \(\kappa\), over every test scheme.
No separability or reducedness of this finite kernel is assumed.

\begin{lemma}[The geometric primitive and tangent maps]
\label{lem:faithful-geometric-kernel-pair}
The multiplication, identity, and inverse of this kernel endow \(A\)
with a finite commutative cocommutative Hopf algebra structure.
There are injective linear maps
\[
 \begin{split}
 D:\ker R_a&\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_k(A,k),\\
 T:\ker R_{\bar a}&\longrightarrow A
 \end{split}
\]
such that \(D(u)\) is an augmentation derivation, \(T(z)\) is
primitive, and
\[
                 D(u)(T(z))=u^tC_a z .
 \tag{KG2}\label{eq:faithful-actual-mixed-kernel-pair}
\]
Here the right side is the mixed coefficient of the chosen actual
adjoint cubical frame, with no change of its normalization.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We first give the source's construction of the pairing and its
nondegeneracy assertion.

Write
\[
 \mathcal P(x,y)=L_{x+y}\otimes L_x^{-1}\otimes L_y^{-1}\otimes L_0
\]
for the normalized second difference of \(L\).
The actual germ's frame supplies a global generating section \(w_a\)
of
\[
       \mathcal P(x,[\bar a]y)\otimes
                         \mathcal P([a]x,y)^{-1}.
 \tag{KG3}\label{eq:faithful-actual-adjoint-line}
\]
This is its \(\mathrm{frame.ad}(a)\) producer.
The alternating product defining this line is exactly
\(\mathrm{AdSum}([a],[\bar a])\): on arbitrary multiplicative
symbols it expands as
\[
 h(x+[\bar a]y)h(x)^{-1}h([\bar a]y)^{-1}
      h([a]x+y)^{-1}h([a]x)h(y).
\]
Thus no independent polarization comparison is substituted for
the actual generator.

On points \(x\in\ker[a]\), \(v\in\ker[\bar a]\), both second
differences in \eqref{eq:faithful-actual-adjoint-line} have their
canonical axis trivializations.  Evaluation of \(w_a\) in these
trivializations gives a unit \(\chi_a(x,v)\), functorial in the
test algebra.  The value at either axis is \(1\).
The source proves this by comparing the two axis pullbacks with
the identity pullback: their ratio is a global function on the
proper connected group, hence comes from the base, and its value
at the identity is \(1\), as is the constant coefficient of
\(g.B.\mathrm{ad}(a)\).
The same global-function comparison, applied to translation by a
kernel point, gives
\[
 \chi_a(y+x,v)=\chi_a(y,v)\chi_a(x,v)
              \qquad([a]x=0,\ [\bar a]v=0).
 \tag{KG4}\label{eq:faithful-kernel-character-law}
\]
More explicitly, compare the two pullbacks of \(w_a\) indexed by
\((y+x,v)\) and \((y,v)\).  Their alternating line expressions
agree because \([a]x=[\bar a]v=0\); the ratio is constant in the
proper group variable \(y\), and evaluating at \(y=0\) gives the
factor \(\chi_a(x,v)\).  These are the source's
\texttt{pair\_ax} and \texttt{pair\_mul}.

We also need its precise nondegeneracy argument, only in the
second variable.  If \(R\) is local and \(v\in G(R)\) is killed by
\([\bar a]\), suppose
\[
      \chi_a(x,v)=1
 \quad\text{for every point }x\in\ker[a]
 \text{ after every base change over }\operatorname{Spec}R.
 \tag{KG5}\label{eq:faithful-kernel-pair-trivial}
\]
Then \(v=0\).
First \([a]:G\to G\) is finite, flat, and surjective in this
situation.  Indeed it is proper; its geometric kernel points
factor through the finite scheme \(\operatorname{Spec}A\), and
each geometric fibre is a translate of that kernel.  Thus it is
locally quasi-finite and proper, hence finite.  The source's
finite-endomorphism result for a smooth geometrically integral
proper group gives flatness and surjectivity.  In particular it
is an fpqc cover, also after base change to \(R\).

On \(G_R\), put \(\mathcal P_v=\mathcal P(-,v)\).
The inverse of \(w_a(-,v)\) is a generator of
\([a]^*\mathcal P_v\).  Two points with the same image under
\([a]\) differ by a kernel point.  Equations
\eqref{eq:faithful-kernel-character-law} and
\eqref{eq:faithful-kernel-pair-trivial} therefore say that these
two pullbacks of that generator agree.  Faithfully flat descent
of a generating section and its inverse gives a generator of
\(\mathcal P_v\).  The line \(L_v\otimes L_0^{-1}\) over the local
base \(\operatorname{Spec}R\) has a generator.  Multiplying it
with the descended generator gives a generator of
\(t_v^*L\otimes L^{-1}\) on \(G_R\).
The stipulated \(\mathrm{KTriv}(G,L)\) now gives \(v=0\).
This is \texttt{pair\_nondeg}; it is a descent of the actual
adjoint-frame generator, not an invocation of an unspecified
duality pairing.

We construct the linear maps in finite bases, as in
\texttt{ker\_data}.  Choose bases
\(u_1,\ldots,u_s\) of \(\ker R_a\) and
\(v_1,\ldots,v_r\) of \(\ker R_{\bar a}\).
For any \(n\), let
\[
     S_n=k\oplus k^n,\qquad (0\oplus k^n)^2=0 .
\]
The actual germ sends the coordinate tuple
\((0,\,(u_j)_l)_{l=1}^d\) to a point over \(S_s\);
since its linear \([a]\)-image is zero, that point factors
uniquely through \(\operatorname{Spec}A\).
In terms of functions this is an algebra map
\[
       \psi:A\longrightarrow S_s,\qquad
       \psi(b)=\bigl(\epsilon(b),(D_j(b))_{j=1}^s\bigr).
\]
Its constant component is the identity point \(\epsilon\):
reduce the point modulo the square-zero ideal and use uniqueness
of its kernel factorization.  Multiplicativity of \(\psi\)
therefore gives
\[
 D_j(bb')=\epsilon(b)D_j(b')+D_j(b)\epsilon(b').
 \tag{KG6}\label{eq:faithful-kernel-derivation}
\]
A linear relation \(\sum c_jD_j=0\), composed with the map
\(S_s\to k[\delta]/(\delta^2)\) determined by
\((e_j)\mapsto c_j\delta\), says that the actual first-order
point in direction \(\sum c_j u_j\) is zero.
The actual germ's first-order coordinate injectivity makes that
direction zero; independence of the \(u_j\) gives all \(c_j=0\).

Likewise the directions \(v_i\) give a point
\(z\in\ker[\bar a](S_r)\).
Apply \(\chi_a\) to the universal point
\(\kappa\in\ker[a](A)\) and this point over
\(A\otimes_k S_r\).  The resulting unit has the form
\[
             c=1+\sum_{i=1}^r f_i\otimes e_i .
 \tag{KG7}\label{eq:faithful-kernel-character-coefficients}
\]
Its constant coefficient is \(1\) by the axis normalization.
Let \(\Delta:A\to A\otimes_kA\) be obtained by multiplying the
two universal kernel points.  Formula
\eqref{eq:faithful-kernel-character-law} gives
\[
   (\Delta\otimes1)c=c_{13}c_{23}.
\]
Comparison of the square-zero coefficients in \(S_r\) gives
\[
                   \Delta(f_i)=f_i\otimes1+1\otimes f_i.
 \tag{KG8}\label{eq:faithful-kernel-primitives}
\]
If \(\sum_i c_i f_i=0\), substitute the direction map
\(S_r\to k[\delta]/(\delta^2)\) into
\eqref{eq:faithful-kernel-character-coefficients}.
The resulting character is identically \(1\), universally on
\(\ker[a]\).  The nondegeneracy just proved gives that the
first-order point with direction \(\sum_i c_i v_i\) is zero.
First-order coordinate injectivity and independence of the
\(v_i\) show that every \(c_i=0\).

It remains to check the normalization in
\eqref{eq:faithful-actual-mixed-kernel-pair}.
Evaluate the adjoint frame on the two first-order points over
\(S_s\otimes_kS_r\).
The germ coordinates have zero products within the first
square-zero block and within the second.  Therefore the formal
series \(g.B.\mathrm{ad}(a)(X,Y)\) has only its constant, pure
first-order, and mixed terms at this point.  Its restrictions
to either axis are \(1\), so the pure terms vanish.
The coefficient of \(e_j\otimes e_i\) is exactly
\[
                    \sum_{l,m}(u_j)_l(C_a)_{lm}(v_i)_m.
\]
On the other hand substituting \(\psi\) into
\eqref{eq:faithful-kernel-character-coefficients} gives that same
coefficient as \(D_j(f_i)\).
The actual frame-to-series comparison is
\texttt{chi\_chart}; the coefficient extraction is
\texttt{second\_jet\_expansion}.  This proves the equality with
the actual \(C_a\), including its sign and transpose convention.

Finally the kernel's multiplication, identity, and inverse factor
uniquely through itself.  On coordinate rings these give
\(\Delta,\epsilon,\iota\); uniqueness of kernel factorization
transfers associativity, the identity laws, inversion, and
commutativity to the Hopf laws.  Equivalently this is the
source's \texttt{exists\_hopf} construction on the same underlying
algebra \(A\).  Extend \(u_j\mapsto D_j\) and \(v_i\mapsto f_i\)
linearly from the chosen bases.  Their independence gives
injectivity; equations \eqref{eq:faithful-kernel-derivation},
\eqref{eq:faithful-kernel-primitives}, and the mixed calculation
give all the asserted identities.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The proper-germ kernel divisibility]
\label{prop:faithful-proper-germ-kernel-divisibility}
Under the same hypotheses,
\[
       p^{\,\dim_k\ker\rho_H(a)}\mid\dim_k A .
 \tag{KG9}\label{eq:faithful-proper-germ-kernel-divisibility}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set
\[
 W_a=\{u\in\ker R_a:
                  z^tC_a^{\,t}u=0\text{ for all }z\in\ker R_{\bar a}\}.
\]
Project \(\ker\rho_H(a)\) to its first component.
If \((u,f)\) lies in this kernel, then \(R_a u=0\) and
\(C_a^tu=R_{\bar a}^tf\).
For \(z\in\ker R_{\bar a}\) this gives
\(z^tC_a^tu=(R_{\bar a}z)^tf=0\), so the projection's image
is contained in \(W_a\).
Its kernel injects by the second component into
\(\ker R_{\bar a}^t\).
The kernel dimensions of a square matrix and its transpose agree.
Rank--nullity consequently gives the exact bound used by the source:
\[
       \dim_k\ker\rho_H(a)
             \le \dim_k\ker R_{\bar a}+\dim_k W_a.
 \tag{KG10}\label{eq:faithful-doubled-kernel-bound}
\]
No equality or unproved lifting of a first component is needed.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:faithful-geometric-kernel-pair}.
The primitive subspace \(P=T(\ker R_{\bar a})\) has dimension
\(\dim_k\ker R_{\bar a}\), and the augmentation-derivation
subspace \(W=D(W_a)\) has dimension \(\dim_kW_a\).
Equation \eqref{eq:faithful-actual-mixed-kernel-pair} and the
definition of \(W_a\) make these spaces orthogonal.
Proposition~\ref{prop:faithful-primitive-tangent-count} yields
\[
 p^{\,\dim_k\ker R_{\bar a}+\dim_kW_a}\mid\dim_kA.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:faithful-doubled-kernel-bound} implies
\eqref{eq:faithful-proper-germ-kernel-divisibility}.
\end{proof}
