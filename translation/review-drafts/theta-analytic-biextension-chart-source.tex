\begin{srlemma}[A generator whose Hermitian forms cancel]
\label{lem:ftb-generator-forms-cancel}
Let \(q:\mathbb C^N\to Z(\mathbb C)\) be regular-evaluation-holomorphic.
Suppose \(\phi_k:Z\to G_y\), integer exponents \(\epsilon_k\),
linear maps \(A_k:\mathbb C^N\to\mathbb C^g\), and an actual
generating section \(s\) of \(\bigotimes_k\phi_k^*L^{\epsilon_k}\)
satisfy \(\phi_kq=eA_k\).
Suppose a group of periods \(\Gamma\subset\mathbb C^N\)
spans the underlying real space,
\(q(z+\gamma)=q(z)\), \(A_k\gamma\) belongs to the period group
of \(e\), and
\[
                    \sum_k\epsilon_kH(A_kz,A_kw)=0
                                            \quad(z,w\in\mathbb C^N).
                                                               \tag{AB1}
\]
Then there is one nonzero constant \(v\) such that the local
reading of \(s(q(z))\) is
\[
                        s_{\mathbf j}(q(z))
                           =v\prod_k f_{j_k}(A_kz)^{\epsilon_k}.
                                                               \tag{AB2}
\]
\end{srlemma}

\begin{proof}
The quotient of the left side by the indicated product is
independent of the simultaneous line chart: its transition
factors cancel exactly. It is a nowhere-zero entire function
\(v(z)\), since the generator and all frame factors are nonzero.
Under a period its multiplier is
\[
          \prod_k\chi(A_k\gamma)^{\epsilon_k}
          \exp\!\left(\pi\sum_k\epsilon_kH(A_kz,A_k\gamma)
                   +\frac\pi2\sum_k\epsilon_kH(A_k\gamma,A_k\gamma)\right).
\]
Both sums vanish by (AB1); the remaining multiplier has
absolute value one. Thus \(|v|\) is \(\Gamma\)-periodic.
Choose a real basis from \(\Gamma\). The lattice generated by
this basis is contained in \(\Gamma\), so its compact
parallelepiped bounds \(|v|\) everywhere.
Liouville's theorem makes \(v\) constant; the nonvanishing
quotient makes its constant nonzero.
This also shows that the displayed product of semicharacters
equals \(1\) for each period.
The proof includes the zero-dimensional case.
\end{proof}

We apply the lemma with the actual maps and their actual
tensor exponents, as follows.

For the cubical generator put
\[
 A_I(z_0,z_1,z_2)=\sum_{i\in I}z_i,\qquad
                       \epsilon_I=(-1)^{3-|I|}.
\]
The third finite difference of the Hermitian quadratic form is
zero:
\[
                   \sum_{I\subset\{0,1,2\}}
                           \epsilon_IH(A_Iz,A_Iw)=0.      \tag{AB3}
\]
One may check this coefficient by coefficient: a diagonal term
with index \(i\) occurs with total coefficient
\(\sum_{I\ni i}(-1)^{3-|I|}=0\), and a mixed term \(i\ne j\)
has coefficient \(\sum_{I\supset\{i,j\}}(-1)^{3-|I|}=0\).
Use the actual triple product \(q=(e(z_0),e(z_1),e(z_2))\);
its product period group spans the real triple space.
Lemma~\ref{lem:ftb-generator-forms-cancel} makes the generator's
reading constant. At the origin, the cubical face normalization
makes its evaluated value \(1\), and
\(\sum_I\epsilon_I=0\) cancels all origin frame factors.
Hence the reading is exactly \(1\) on every triple:
\[
                        \text{the actual cube reads }1.  \tag{AB4}
\]

For the primitive comparison, the actual line is
\[
                             L_x B(x,Tx)^{-1}.           \tag{AB5}
\]
The source first constructs a generator from \(t.\mathrm{prim}\)
by inverting its division-line generator.
Restricting it to the identity gives a generating comparison
with the given rigidification; their ratio is a unit on the
base. Multiply by the inverse of this pulled-back base unit.
This produces the literal normalized \(w_P\), with
\(\mathrm{IsPrim}(w_P)\); the orientation in (AB5) is unchanged.

The actual host identity is
\(H.w_0+\overline{H.w_0}=1\).
Indeed \(2H.w_0=1+H.rt\), \(H.rt^2=-H.d\), and complex
conjugation sends \(H.rt\) to \(-H.rt\).
The latter follows by factoring the equality of squares:
the alternative that \(H.rt\) is real would give a real
square equal to the negative integer \(-H.d\).
Thus \(T+T_{\overline{H.w_0}}=1\), and the adjoint law in (AJ1)
gives
\[
 H(z,w)-H(z,Tw)-H(Tz,w)=0.                               \tag{AB6}
\]
This is the sum of Hermitian forms with the exact exponents of
(AB5), obtained by expanding
\(H(z+Tz,w+Tw)-H(z,w)-H(Tz,Tw)\).
The maps \(T_a\) preserve the period group, because they lift
the actual algebraic action. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:ftb-generator-forms-cancel} on the actual
\(e(z)\) to \(w_P\). Its constant is \(1\):
at the origin the restriction of \(w_P\) is the rigidification,
while the exponent sum of (AB5) is \(1\), so its product of
origin frame values is exactly that same rigidification.
We have therefore proved
\[
                         \text{the actual }w_P\text{ reads }1.
                                                               \tag{AB7}
\]
This holds for every \(w_P\) satisfying the specified
\(\mathrm{IsPrim}\) condition.

Finally, for any \(a\in\mathcal O_E\), the Rosati comparison
has line
\[
                              B(x,T_{\bar a}y)B(T_ax,y)^{-1}.
                                                               \tag{AB8}
\]
Use its actual algebraic generator, divided by its base-unit
value at \((0,0)\). Such normalization is allowed because
the two square lines on the axes are canonically trivial.
The Hermitian sum in Lemma~\ref{lem:ftb-generator-forms-cancel}
vanishes by both adjoint identities
\[
 H(T_az,w)=H(z,T_{\bar a}w),\qquad
 H(T_{\bar a}z,w)=H(z,T_aw).
\]
The product period group and \((0,0)\)-normalization give an
actual generator \(w_a\) of (AB8) with reading \(1\).
This is the source \srcid{exists_adj_reading_one};
no numerical scalar or phase remains to be chosen later.

\begin{srlemma}[Actual equality from normalized jet readings]
\label{lem:ftb-actual-jet-reading-corr}
Suppose two products of actual jet-line generators have
\(\mathrm{JetRd}\) readings of scalar \(1\), and their signed
lists of actual maps have the same sum, including multiplicity.
They correspond under the original \(\mathrm{LB.SameSum}\)
tensor identification.
This statement applies to the immersive exponential above.
It also commutes with every displayed complex linear
substitution of the jet variables.
\end{srlemma}

\begin{proof}
We include the order issue in the original proof.
For \(m=0\), all jet products are products of the origin frame
values; the signed multiplicities identify them.
For \(m\geq1\), if the same actual jet map admits two linear
lifts \(A,A'\), its first-order regular evaluations agree.
The linear Taylor coefficients are the derivatives of
\(a(e(Az))\) and \(a(e(A'z))\).
By the chain rule their difference is
\(d(a\circ e)_0((A-A')z)\).
Immersion (AJ6) therefore gives \(A=A'\).
Thus whenever an actual jet map occurs twice in the signed
lists, its corresponding frame jet is the same.

On a nonempty coherent common chart, assign the same line-chart
index to every occurrence of the same actual map, as in the
definition of \(\mathrm{LB.Gen.Corr}\). All frame jets are units.
Group their powers by the actual map they label.
The exponent sum for each such map is the same on the two
sides by \(\mathrm{LB.SameSum}\); the products of units are
therefore equal. This is equality of the generators'
actual local sections by (AJ7). Empty chart intersections
give the unique empty-open equality.

For a linear substitution, Taylor substitution sends each
coordinate \(Z_{(i,j)}\) to its specified linear polynomial.
It commutes with products, inverses and the variable renaming
\(\mathrm{renN}\). The pulled generator therefore has the
same reading after composing all its linear lifts with that
linear map. Applying the preceding argument proves the
asserted compatibility.
\end{proof}

Let \(e_m(x,y)\) be the actual pair-line generators (AJ8),
whose scalar jet reading is \(1\).
All expressions below mean their pullbacks to the indicated
common jet algebra.
The first and second partial laws are
\[
 \begin{split}
 C(x,y,z)&\ \longleftrightarrow\
          e_m(x+y,z)e_m(x,z)^{-1}e_m(y,z)^{-1},\\
 C(y,z,x)&\ \longleftrightarrow\
          e_m(x,y+z)e_m(x,y)^{-1}e_m(x,z)^{-1}.
 \end{split}                                             \tag{AB9}
\]
Here \(C\) denotes the specified algebraic cubical generator,
and the cyclic order in the second line is the one used in
the original definition of \(\mathrm{IsFrame}\).
To verify the signed map lists, expand
\(B(x+y,z)B(x,z)^{-1}B(y,z)^{-1}\):
its exponents are \(+1\) at \(x+y+z,x,y,z\),
and \(-1\) at \(x+y,x+z,y+z,0\).
The second line gives the same expansion in the displayed
cyclic order. The actual cube has reading \(1\) by (AB4);
all pair terms have reading \(1\) by linear substitution in
(AJ7). Lemma~\ref{lem:ftb-actual-jet-reading-corr} proves
both original correspondences.

Likewise the variable swap preserves the signed subsets
of the pair line, so
\[
                             e_m(x,y)\longleftrightarrow e_m(y,x).
                                                               \tag{AB10}
\]
For adjointness, use the actual \(w_a\) from (AB8).
Its reading and the readings of the two substituted pair
generators are \(1\), and their signed map lists cancel:
\[
                    w_a(x,y)e_m(T_ax,y)
                                  \longleftrightarrow e_m(x,T_{\bar a}y).
                                                               \tag{AB11}
\]
This verifies the exact \(\mathrm{IsFrame.ad}\) orientation.
Consequently the formal biextension data on the additive
formal group have
\[
                    \mathrm{ph1}=\mathrm{ph2}=\mathrm{sy}=1,
                       \qquad \mathrm{ad}(a)=1.           \tag{AB12}
\]
The unit witnesses in the finite-jet definitions are literally
\(1\), not unspecified units with constant term \(1\).

\begin{srproposition}[The actual additive-gauge chart and Taylor germs]
\label{prop:ftb-actual-analytic-chart-taylor}
There is one actual chart \(C\) at \(y\), chosen before
\(k,s,\theta\), with
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 C.d=g,\qquad C.B.G.F_i(Z,W)=Z_i+W_i,\qquad C.h=0,\\
                       C.uvec_\ell=e_{\mathrm{el}^{-1}\ell},
 \end{gathered}                                           \tag{AB13}
\]
whose jet points are the actual \(x_m\) constructed from \(e\).
For every \(k\geq0\) and every actual section \(s\) read by
\(\theta\), the formal germ in this chart is precisely
\[
                              f=\operatorname{tay}(\theta,0).
                                                               \tag{AB14}
\]
In particular \(C\) is a witness of the original
\(\mathrm{ChartAt}\), and (AB14) means its full
\(\mathrm{IsGerm}\) condition, including line-generator
and actual section-pullback comparisons.
\end{srproposition}

\begin{proof}
Take \(C.B\) to be the additive formal group with diagonal
\(\mathcal O_E\)-action and the trivial series (AB12).
Take its \(x_m,e_m,w_P\) to be the single choices above.
Its group-chart clauses are
Lemma~\ref{lem:ftb-actual-jet-formal-chart};
its frame clauses are (AB9)--(AB12);
its primitive clause is the actual normalized (AB5).
For this trivial additive biextension, the zero series
\(h=0\) satisfies the canonical-gauge equations: substituting
the additive law and the constant series \(1\) makes every
multiplicative defect \(1\) and every logarithmic derivative
zero. This is the original \(\mathrm{anFB\_canon}\) witness.

It remains to identify the line generator used in
\(\mathrm{IsGerm}\).
The source defines it by the literal product
\[
                   \ell_m=w_P(x_m)\,e_m(x_m,Tx_m),        \tag{AB15}
\]
the cancellation of (AB5) with the pair line on the graph
of \(T\). Its tensor line is \(x_m^*L\).
By (AB7) and the linear substitution \(z\mapsto(z,Tz)\),
its local jet reading is exactly \(j_mf_j\).
Thus, for \(k\geq0\), any generator \(\tau_m\) from
Lemma~\ref{lem:ftb-actual-jet-generator} with reading
\(j_m(f_j^k)\) corresponds to \(\ell_m^k\), by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ftb-actual-jet-reading-corr}.
At \(m=0\), augmentation of (AB15) agrees with the actual
rigidification: its exponent sum is \(1\), and all factors
have their specified origin values. This is the required
\(\mathrm{ChartAt.rig0}\), not a rescaling made separately
for each section.

Choose \(s_m\) to be the actual pullback of \(s\), as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:ftb-actual-section-jet-value}.
That lemma gives
\(s_m(x_m)=(j_m\theta)\tau_m\), while the preceding paragraph
gives the required correspondence
\(\tau_m\longleftrightarrow\ell_m^k\).
Finally \(j_m\theta\) is exactly the truncation of
\(\operatorname{tay}(\theta,0)\).
These are all three clauses of the original
\(\mathrm{IsGerm}\), for every \(m\).
The choices \(x_m,e_m,w_P,h\) did not depend on \(k,s,\theta\).
This proves the simultaneous assertion (AB14).
\end{proof}
