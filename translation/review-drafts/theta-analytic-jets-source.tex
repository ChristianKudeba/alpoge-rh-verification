\subsection{Actual exponential jets and their frame generators}
\label{subsec:ftb-actual-exponential-jets}

We work at the same complex framed point \(y\), exponential \(e\),
and analytic frames \(f_j\) as in the preceding comparison.
More precisely the input is the original \(\mathrm{AnPt}(y,g,\mathrm{ty},\mathrm{el})\).
Thus \(e:\mathbb C^g\to G_y(\mathbb C)\) is additive and surjective,
the regular-function evaluations along \(e\) are holomorphic,
and the nonvanishing \(f_j\) have the actual algebraic line
transition functions, the specified origin rigidification and
Appell--Humbert factors.
For each \(a\in\mathcal O_E\),
\[
 e(\mathrm{tyAct}(a)z)=a\,e(z),\qquad
 H(\mathrm{tyAct}(a)z,w)=H(z,\mathrm{tyAct}(\bar a)w).
                                                               \tag{AJ1}
\]
The derivative in the coordinate direction
\(e_{\mathrm{el}^{-1}\ell}\) is the original algebraic tangent
vector \(y.u_\ell\), and these vectors form the actual Lie basis.
This is an input tuple at this stage; the preceding fixed-fibre
construction and the relative dictionary are its separate producers.

For a finite set \(I\), put
\[
 J_m(I)=\mathbb C[Z_i:i\in I]/(Z_i:i\in I)^{m+1}.
\]
Its augmentation ideal is nilpotent and its residue field is
\(\mathbb C\). It has just one prime ideal: every prime contains
that nilpotent ideal, and the quotient is a field.
The constant term detects units:
\[
                        u\in J_m(I)^\times
                         \Longleftrightarrow u(0)\ne0.    \tag{AJ2}
\]
For a holomorphic germ \(h\) at \(0\), let \(j_mh\) be its
Taylor polynomial modulo degree \(m+1\), with the usual factorials.
Taylor multiplication and the local inverse identity give
\[
 j_m(hh')=(j_mh)(j_mh'),\quad
 j_m(h^{-1})=(j_mh)^{-1}\quad(h(0)\ne0).                   \tag{AJ3}
\]
For an integer exponent \(\epsilon\), this also proves
\(j_m(h^\epsilon)=(j_mh)^\epsilon\).
All uses of inverse powers below are covered by this nonvanishing
condition.

\begin{srlemma}[The actual jet points and their linear substitutions]
\label{lem:ftb-actual-exponential-jet-points}
There is a unique jet point \(x_m:\operatorname{Spec}J_m(\{0\}\times[g])
\to G_y\) whose value on every regular function \(a\) on an
origin chart is
\[
                         x_m^*a=j_m(a(e(z))).             \tag{AJ4}
\]
It is exactly the source \(\Phi.jx(m)\).
These points commute with truncation and have augmentation equal
to the identity. For any \(n\), and any complex linear
\(T:(\mathbb C^g)^n\to\mathbb C^g\), let \(x_{T,m}\) be the
point defined by \(j_m(a(e(Tz)))\).
Then, as actual scheme maps on the same jet algebra,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 x_{0,m}&=0,&
 x_{T,m}+x_{T',m}&=x_{T+T',m},\\
 a\,x_{T,m}&=x_{\mathrm{tyAct}(a)T,m},&
 x_{\mathrm{pr}_i,m}&=\text{the \(i\)-th copy of }x_m.
 \end{aligned}                                             \tag{AJ5}
\]
In multiplicative notation for the algebraic group, the zeros
and sums in this display are its identity and products.
\end{srlemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose an affine open containing \(e(0)\).
The map from its coordinate ring in (AJ4) is a ring map by (AJ3).
If a chart denominator has nonzero value at \(e(0)\), its jet
has nonzero constant term, hence is invertible by (AJ2).
The map therefore localizes correctly and is independent of the
chosen chart. Since the jet spectrum has one point, every
origin chart contains the whole jet image; equality there
proves uniqueness as a map to the original scheme.
Taylor truncation proves compatibility in \(m\), and the
constant-term map evaluates at \(e(0)=0\).

For the substitution by \(T\), one can identify
\((\mathbb C^g)^n\) with \(\mathbb C^{ng}\) in the source's
fixed order, apply (AJ4), and rename the variables by
\((i,j)\mapsto\operatorname{finProdFinEquiv}(i,j)\).
The source's map \(\mathrm{renN}\) is precisely this renaming.
In particular copying the \(i\)-th column sends \(Z_j\) to
\(Z_{(i,j)}\), with no scaling.
The chain rule for a linear substitution gives the same result
as direct Taylor substitution.

Products of the two analytic maps \(e(Tz),e(T'z)\) are
holomorphic as maps to the actual fibre product:
on an affine product chart every regular function is a
local quotient of finite sums of products of functions from
the two factors. Taylor multiplication proves that its jet
has the stated two jet points as its two projections.
The fibre-product universal property identifies this jet with
their algebraic pair.
Composing with the algebraic group multiplication and using
\(e(Tz)e(T'z)=e((T+T')z)\) proves the middle identity of (AJ5).
The identity and copying statements are direct substitutions.
The \(\mathcal O_E\)-action statement follows by composing with
the actual algebraic action and using (AJ1).
Thus (AJ5) is a statement about actual jets of the given
exponential, rather than formal points with unrelated tangent
vectors.
\end{proof}

\begin{srlemma}[The actual differential and formal-group chart]
\label{lem:ftb-actual-jet-formal-chart}
The points \(x_m\), additive formal group law
\(F_i(Z,W)=Z_i+W_i\), diagonal action
\(Z_i\mapsto\mathrm{ty}(i)(a)Z_i\), and directions
\[
                  u_\ell=e_{\mathrm{el}^{-1}\ell}
\]
satisfy the original \(\mathrm{IsChart}\) conditions.
Moreover the exponential is immersive in the exact
regular-evaluation sense: for every \(w\ne0\), some regular
function on an actual identity chart has
\[
                         d(a\circ e)_0(w)\ne0.           \tag{AJ6}
\]
\end{srlemma}

\begin{proof}
The augmentation, truncation, group-law and action clauses
are (AJ4)--(AJ5).
Evaluating \(x_1\) at
\(Z_j\mapsto\delta_{ji}\varepsilon\) gives the derivative
of \(e\) in the \(i\)-th coordinate.
By the \(\mathrm{AnPt.der}\) clause these are the actual
\(y.u_\ell\), in exactly the order given by \(\mathrm{el}\).
The one nonzero coefficient in a coordinate combination is
therefore the required tangent clause; the coordinate vectors
give the required basis clause.

To prove (AJ6), suppose all regular-evaluation derivatives
annihilate \(w\). Then the dual-number point associated to
\(w\) equals the identity tangent vector: their affine
coordinate-ring homomorphisms agree, and localization makes
the statement independent of the affine identity chart.
For completeness, additivity of \(e\) makes the derivative
of a sum the product of the corresponding dual-number
points; scaling \(w\) scales those points.
Write \(w\) in the ordered coordinate basis.
Its tangent point is consequently the linear combination
of the \(y.u_\ell\) with coefficients
\(w_{\mathrm{el}^{-1}\ell}\).
The actual Lie-basis uniqueness at \(y\) makes all those
coefficients zero, so \(w=0\), a contradiction.
This is the source's \(\mathrm{anPt\_imm}\) argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{srlemma}[The actual tensor-line generator at the jets]
\label{lem:ftb-actual-jet-generator}
Let \(K\) be finite, let \(\epsilon_k\in\mathbb Z\), and suppose
\(\phi_k=x_{T_k,m}\) as actual maps, with each \(T_k\) complex
linear as above. The tensor line
\[
                        \bigotimes_{k\in K}\phi_k^*L^{\epsilon_k}
\]
has a generating section \(\tau\) whose local value is exactly
\[
                   \tau_{\mathbf j}
                       =j_m\!\left(\prod_{k\in K}
                                      f_{j_k}(T_kz)^{\epsilon_k}\right)
                                                               \tag{AJ7}
\]
on every nonempty simultaneous line chart.
This is the original \(\mathrm{JetRd}(m,\phi,\epsilon,\tau,T)\).
\end{srlemma}

\begin{proof}
A nonempty chart of the jet spectrum is its whole one-point
spectrum. For each \(k,j\) occurring on such a chart,
\(f_j(T_k0)\ne0\); hence \(j_m(f_j(T_kz))\) is a unit.
On two simultaneous charts the analytic frame identity is
\[
 f_j(T_kz)=g_{jj'}(e(T_kz))\,f_{j'}(T_kz).
\]
Take its Taylor jet, using (AJ3), and then the product with
integer exponents \(\epsilon_k\).
The resulting equality is exactly the transition rule for
the tensor line, so the products in (AJ7) glue to a generator.
On an empty chart the sections and units have their unique
empty-open restrictions; this supplies the unused chart
components without a nonvanishing assertion there.
This is the source's construction
\srcid{exists_gen_of_units}.
In its global choice convention a unit is chosen from the
nonzero constant term, and the value \(1\) is used on an
unused chart; the resulting sections on all nonempty charts
are still literally (AJ7).

Taylor jets of products and negative powers are precisely
(AJ3), so there is no unspecified scalar in (AJ7).
The point identifications required by \(\mathrm{JetRd}\)
are the previously proved (AJ5).
\end{proof}

In particular use \(K=\mathcal P(\{0,1\})\),
\(T_I(z_0,z_1)=\sum_{i\in I}z_i\), and
\(\epsilon_I=(-1)^{2-|I|}\).
The tensor line in Lemma~\ref{lem:ftb-actual-jet-generator} has
fibres
\[
                L_{z_0+z_1}L_0L_{z_0}^{-1}L_{z_1}^{-1}.
                                                               \tag{AJ8}
\]
For each \(m\), (AJ7) gives a generator \(e_m\) of this exact
line, with scalar reading \(1\).
These are the actual \(\mathrm{FrTy}\) and
\(\mathrm{IsAnE}\) witnesses.
They are chosen for each \(m\); compatibility of the
generators \(e_m\) with truncation is not asserted here.
The later frame-correction argument treats that separate
condition. The jet points \(x_m\) themselves are already
compatible.

\begin{srlemma}[Every section has its actual entire reading]
\label{lem:ftb-actual-section-entire-reading}
For every integer \(k\), an actual section \(s\) of \(L^k\)
on \(G_y\) has a unique reading \(\theta_s\), characterized by
\[
                       s_j(e(z))=\theta_s(z)f_j(z)^k.     \tag{AJ9}
\]
It is entire and satisfies the exact Appell--Humbert
transformation of weight \(k\).
These statements use the given analytic frame even if \(k<0\).
\end{srlemma}

\begin{proof}
Where \(e(z)\) lies in the \(j\)-th chart set
\(\theta_s(z)=s_j(e(z))/f_j(z)^k\).
The section and frame transitions cancel, so the quotients
agree on every overlap and define one function.
They are holomorphic and their denominators are nonzero,
so the function is entire. This also proves uniqueness.

To interpret the formula on the actual pullback group
\(G_y\), use the point
\(\widetilde e(z)=(y,e(z))\) of the fibre product.
It has the specified two projections and is unique with
these projections. Its regular-function evaluations are
holomorphic by the same affine product-chart argument used
in Lemma~\ref{lem:ftb-actual-exponential-jet-points}.
Thus all evaluations in (AJ9) are evaluations of the
original algebraic section on its specified pullback,
as required by \(\mathrm{AnFr.Rd}\).
A lattice translation leaves \(\widetilde e(z)\) unchanged.
Substitute the actual frame automorphy into (AJ9) and
cancel the nonzero \(f_j(z+\lambda)^k\).
This gives
\[
             \theta_s(z+\lambda)
                 =\left(\chi(\lambda)
                   e^{\pi H(z,\lambda)+\pi H(\lambda,\lambda)/2}\right)^k
                       \theta_s(z),
\]
including negative integer powers.
\end{proof}

\begin{srlemma}[Section value at the actual jet point]
\label{lem:ftb-actual-section-jet-value}
Let \(k\geq0\), let \(s\) be a section read by \(\theta_s\)
as above, and let \(s_m\) be its actual pullback to
\(\operatorname{Spec}J_m\times G_y\).
Let \(\tau_m\) be a generator of \(x_m^*L^k\) satisfying
the single-factor form of (AJ7), namely local value
\(j_m(f_j^k)\). Then
\[
                         s_m(x_m)=(j_m\theta_s)\tau_m     \tag{AJ10}
\]
as sections on the actual jet spectrum.
\end{srlemma}

\begin{proof}
The point-section map of \(x_m\), followed by the base-change
map, is exactly the jet of \(\widetilde e(z)\): both
projections agree, by (AJ4) and the fibre-product universal
property. Pullback of \(s\) therefore has, on a chart
containing its unique point, value
\(j_m(s_j(e(z)))\).
Equation (AJ9) holds on a neighborhood of \(0\); taking
Taylor jets and applying (AJ3) gives
\[
             j_m(s_j(e(z)))=(j_m\theta_s)\,j_m(f_j^k).
\]
The last factor is the local value of \(\tau_m\).
This proves (AJ10) on every nonempty chart and hence as a
section. Equivalently it is the original
\(\mathrm{IsValueAt}\) equality, including its actual
\(\mathrm{IsPullSec}\) comparison.

For the algebraic pullback used here, one can write its
local sections directly by pulling \(s_j\) along the
whiskered base-change map. Pullback commutes with the line
transitions, so they glue, and their local values are the
ones just used. Thus no section-base-change existence
package is being substituted for this actual pullback.
\end{proof}
