\subsection{Reconstructing the actual morphism from bounded graphs}
\label{subsec:ftb-bounded-graph-reconstruction}

The next lemma records the properness and flatness argument needed
to turn the reconstructed projective graph into a morphism.
It is important here that the good primes satisfy
\eqref{eq:ftb-very-dense-primes}.

\begin{srlemma}[A proper flat fibrewise-isomorphism test]
\label{lem:ftb-proper-flat-good-fibre-iso}
Let \(R\) be Noetherian, let \(X/R\) be proper and flat, and
let \(f:\Gamma\to X\) be proper with \(\Gamma/R\) flat.
Suppose a very dense set \(P\) has, at each \(p\in P\),
a field map \(x:R\to K\) with kernel \(p\) for which
\(f_K\) is an isomorphism.
Then \(f\) is an isomorphism.
\end{srlemma}
\begin{proof}
We first explain how a property local on the target is
promoted from neighborhoods over the good primes.
Let \(W\subset X\) be the union of open sets on which
that property holds.
If \(W\ne X\), properness makes the image in
\(\operatorname{Spec}R\) of the closed complement \(X\setminus W\)
closed.
For a point of that image, choose a good prime above it
by \eqref{eq:ftb-very-dense-primes}, taking \(f=1\)
there.  The good prime remains in the closed image,
contradicting the assumed local property over that good
fibre.
Thus neighborhoods of all points over the good primes
suffice.

First apply this argument to finiteness.
For a point \(z\in X\) over a good prime, pull to the
given field \(K\).
Since \(f_K\) is an isomorphism, the fibre over \(z\)
has at most one point.
A proper morphism with finite fibre at \(z\) is finite
over a neighborhood of \(z\).
Thus the preceding argument makes \(f\) finite.

On a target affine chart, write it as a finite ring map
\(A\to B\).
At a point of a good fibre the base-changed map is
surjective.
Its finite cokernel \(C=B/A\) has zero fibre there.
The support criterion for a finite module gives
\(a\in\operatorname{Ann}_A C\) not vanishing at that
point.  After inverting \(a\), the ring map is surjective.
Thus \(f\) is a closed immersion near every point of
every good fibre, and the preceding argument makes it
a closed immersion globally.

Now \(I=\ker(A\to B)\) is finite because \(A\) is
Noetherian.
The local ring \(B\) is flat over \(R\), since its
scheme is an open subset of \(\Gamma\).
Tensoring \(0\to I\to A\to B\to0\) with the field \(K\)
therefore preserves injectivity on the left.
Injectivity of the good fibre ring map makes
\(I\otimes_RK=0\).
At the chosen point above this fibre, finite generation
again supplies an annihilator \(a\) not vanishing there.
The localized map has zero kernel and is already
surjective; it is an isomorphism.
A final application of the open-set argument proves
the lemma.
The argument uses flatness of the source to remove
the closed-immersion kernel; properness and good
set-theoretic fibres alone would not suffice.
\end{proof}

For a projective coordinate representation
\((Z_i,M_i,\vartheta_i)\), and a fibre morphism
\(f:(Z_1)_K\to(Z_2)_K\), define its homogeneous
\emph{graph ideal} to be the polynomials vanishing on
every product coordinate vector
\[
                  (v_{1,i}v_{2,j})_{(i,j)}
\]
obtained on any \(K\)-algebra test scheme from the two
actual line-section coordinates along \(1\) and \(f\).
This is the source's \(\mathrm{graphIdeal}\).
Equivalently it is the homogeneous ideal of the graph
under the Segre coordinate representation.
The graph is closed because \(Z_2\) is separated; its
coordinate line is \(M_1\otimes f^*M_2\) on \((Z_1)_K\).

\begin{srproposition}[The actual same-degree bounded graph producer]
\label{prop:ftb-morphism-from-bounded-graphs}
Let \(R\) be reduced and Noetherian.
Let \(Z_1,Z_2\) be proper \(R\)-schemes with projective
coordinate representations
\((M_i,\vartheta_{i,a})\), indexed by finite sets
\(\Sigma_i\), and assume \(Z_1/R\) smooth.
Fix a finite label set \(\mathcal H\), vectors
\(p_{i,h}\in R^{\Sigma_i}\), one degree \(d\), and one
polynomial \(Q\in\mathbb Q[T]\).
For each field map \(x:R\to K\), let \(G(K,x)\) be a
specified collection of \(K\)-morphisms
\(f:(Z_1)_K\to(Z_2)_K\).
Assume:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{every }f\in G(K,x)\text{ has graph ideal satisfying}\\
 \mathrm{CutBy}\bigl(Q,d,
        (x(p_{1,h,a}p_{2,h,b}))_{h,(a,b)}\bigr),\\
 \{p:\exists K,x,f\in G(K,x),\ \ker x=p\}
                   \text{ is very dense}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{G1}\label{eq:ftb-bounded-graph-data}
\]
Then there is an actual morphism \(F:Z_1\to Z_2\)
whose pullback is every one of the specified \(f\).
It uses exactly the same \(d,Q\) and coordinate products
in \eqref{eq:ftb-bounded-graph-data}.
\end{srproposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:ftb-flat-projective-from-bounded-forms}
to the product vectors
\((p_{1,h,a}p_{2,h,b})_{(a,b)}\).
It gives a projective flat finitely presented
\(\Gamma/R\).
For every specified \(f\), its fibre is identified with
the actual graph of \(f\), with the line and all product
section-coordinate vectors preserved.
In particular \(\Gamma_K\simeq(Z_1)_K\) for every such
good field pullback.

The scheme \(\Gamma/R\) is smooth.
Indeed its nonsmooth locus is closed, and properness
makes its image in \(\operatorname{Spec}R\) closed.
It cannot meet a good prime, since at that prime its
fibre is the smooth scheme \((Z_1)_K\), and a flat
finitely presented map is smooth at a point if its
geometric fibre is smooth there.
If the closed image were nonempty, its stability
under specialization and very density would force it
to meet a good prime.
Thus the nonsmooth locus is empty.
Since \(R\) is reduced, \(\Gamma\) is reduced.

The two coordinate representations give an actual
Segre representation of \(Z_1\times_RZ_2\).
Every homogeneous equation of this product vanishes
on the product coordinates of the good graph fibres.
It therefore vanishes on \(\Gamma\).
Here is the exact density step.
The inverse image of the good prime set is dense:
the flat finitely presented map \(\Gamma\to\operatorname{Spec}R\)
is open, so every nonempty open of \(\Gamma\) maps to
a nonempty open meeting that prime set.
A line section which is zero on all geometric points
over this dense subset is zero on the reduced scheme
\(\Gamma\), by trivializing the line on affine opens
and intersecting their prime ideals.
Apply this to every homogeneous equation.
Its homogeneous components give the same assertion
for an arbitrary polynomial.
Hence the coordinate map of \(\Gamma\) factors through
the product, giving
\[
                              g:\Gamma\longrightarrow Z_1\times_RZ_2.
 \tag{G2}\label{eq:ftb-bounded-graph-product-map}
\]
This is the source's homogeneous-ideal map construction,
not extension of a generically defined rational map.

The product-coordinate comparison identifies
\(g_K\) on every good fibre with the graph embedding
\((1,f)\).
To check this including the line frames, trivialize
both \(M_1,M_2\) on an affine test chart.
Their two coordinate vectors multiply to the Segre
coordinates.
The fibre identification with \(\Gamma_K\) preserves
exactly those products.
Uniqueness in the projective coordinate functor makes
the two maps equal on this chart, and these charts
cover the graph.
Thus the first projection
\[
                              \pi_1:\Gamma\longrightarrow Z_1
\]
has the given fibre isomorphism at every good prime;
the second projection agrees there with \(f\).
The morphism \(\pi_1\) is proper, since \(\Gamma/R\)
is proper and \(Z_1/R\) is separated.
Its source is flat over \(R\), and its target is flat
because it is smooth.
Lemma~\ref{lem:ftb-proper-flat-good-fibre-iso} makes
\(\pi_1\) an isomorphism.
Set \(F=\pi_2\pi_1^{-1}\).
The two projection identities on every specified fibre
give \(F_K=f\), with no change of degree or coordinate
label set.
\end{proof}

This is the exact reconstruction used for the addition,
inversion and specified endomorphisms of the theta family.
Its input there is still substantive: the source must
supply the graph-cut degree and polynomial for the same
torsion labels on every allowed marked abelian fibre.
The proposition proves the algebraic reconstruction after
those data are supplied, and does not silently replace
them by a general extension theorem.
